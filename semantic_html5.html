Overview

The Before Trilogy consists of three romance films directed by Richard Linklater, and starring Ethan Hawke and Julie Delpy. Beginning with Before Sunrise (1995), and continuing with two sequels, Before Sunset (2004) and Before Midnight (2013). The films were all written by Linklater, along with Kim Krizan on the first film, and with Hawke and Delpy on the last two.[a]

Set and filmed at nine-year intervals, the films chronicle the romantic relationship between Jesse (Hawke) and Céline (Delpy) at three periods of their lives. The characters also make cameo appearances in Linklater's animated anthology film Waking Life (2001). The first film was distributed by Columbia Pictures,[b] while Warner Independent Pictures and Sony Pictures Classics distributed the second and third film, respectively. All films were produced by Castle Rock Entertainment, with Before Midnight also being produced by Venture Forth and Linklater's production company, Detour Filmproduction.

The films are considered minimal, consisting mostly of monologues and casual conversation with extended dialogue between the characters. Contrasting ideas and perspectives on life and love are detailed, with the series referencing time, self-discovery, age, loss, gender, and parentage; it is considered an exploration of postmodern romance. The trilogy received widespread critical acclaim. Among its numerous accolades, the series was nominated for two Academy Awards, two Writers Guild of America Awards, and the Golden Globe Award for Best Actress - Motion Picture Comedy or Musical for Delpy for Before Midnight. The trilogy grossed $61.5 million worldwide against a combined budget of $7.5 million.

Films

Before Sunrise

Before Sunrise is set in a single night in Vienna as Jesse, an American student traveling through Europe, and Céline, a French student visiting relatives, meet on a Eurail train to Paris. They wander the streets of Vienna and fall in love, but they go their separate ways and agree to meet again in the future.

Before Sunset

Before Sunset takes place nine years after the first film, and is set in a single afternoon in Paris. Jesse, now a married father and a best-selling author, meets Céline while on tour promoting his latest book, which retells their meeting in Vienna. They wander Paris and lament on not following through with their plans to reunite. At the end of the film, Jesse goes to Céline's apartment, deciding to miss his flight home to stay with her.

Before Midnight

Before Midnight takes place nine years after the second film, and is set in a single day on the Peloponnese coast in Greece. Jesse and Céline, now a couple with twin daughters, argue over Jesse's desire to relocate to Chicago to be closer to his son, Hank, while Céline wishes to stay in Paris to take a job with the French government. Despite their relationship being tested, they eventually reconcile.

Potential fourth film

Linklater, Hawke, and Delpy have discussed continuing the series further, with changing levels of interest.

In March 2020, Hawke stated a sequel may likely not follow the precedent of a nine-year gap between films.

In June 2021, Delpy said that she had turned down going ahead with a sequel, later clarifying that all three of them agreed they could not come up with something good for a fourth one. She said there was some discussion of the direction a fourth film might take, but it was an idea none of them liked.

Production

Before Sunrise

Before Sunrise was inspired by a woman whom Richard Linklater met in a toy shop in Philadelphia in 1989. Due to the prevalence of dialogue, Linklater opted to collaborate on the screenplay with Kim Krizan, who previously appeared in his films Slacker (1990) and Dazed and Confused (1993). Only an outline was discussed, and the screenplay was written in 11 days. Linklater wished to explore relationships and discover two people of complete anonymity and find out who they really were, so he chose a foreign setting.

Casting took nine months. Linklater was originally against casting Ethan Hawke, considering him too young for the part, but hired him after seeing him at a play in New York City. After hiring Julie Delpy, Linklater asked them to read together in Austin, Texas, and then decided they were right for the roles. In 2016, Delpy told Creative Screenwriting that she and Hawke performed uncredited rewrites to bring more romance to the film. Delpy and Hawke subsequently received writing credit for the sequels.

Before Sunset

Linklater considered a larger budget sequel, to be filmed in four locations. His proposal did not secure funding, so he scaled back the concept of the film, with Hawke, Linklater, and Delpy working independently on their own screenplays over the years. They also adapted elements of the earlier scripts for the sequel.

Linklater described completing the final version as a process in which the three worked out a detailed outline together over two or three days, then spent about a year exchanging monologues, dialogue, scenes and ideas by e-mail and fax while he edited, compiled and wrote.

The film was shot entirely on location in Paris. It opens inside the Shakespeare and Company bookstore on the Left Bank. Other locations include the Marais district of the 4th arrondissement, Le Pure Café in the 11th, the Promenade Plantée park in the 12th, a bateau mouche from Quai de la Tournelle to Quai Henri IV, a taxi, and finally Céline's apartment.

Filming took 15 days on a budget of about US$2 million. The film is noted for its use of the Steadicam for tracking shots and long takes, the longest lasting about 11 minutes. The summer was one of the hottest on record, with temperatures exceeding 100 °F (38 °C) for most of the production.

The film essentially takes place in real time, which made it challenging for cinematographer Lee Daniel to match the color and intensity of the skies from scene to scene. The sequel was released nine years after Before Sunrise, the same amount of time that has passed in the plot. It was released in the wake of Hawke's divorce from Uma Thurman, and commentators drew parallels between Hawke's life and the character of Jesse. Delpy wrote two of the songs featured in the film, and a third by her was included in the closing credits and soundtrack.

Before Midnight

In November 2011, Hawke said the three had been talking about revisiting the characters, and that making a film the following summer would again leave nine years between films. In June 2012, Hawke confirmed the sequel would be filmed that summer, and although Delpy soon denied it, reports emerged by August 2012 that the film was being shot in Messenia, Greece. Completion of Before Midnight was announced on September 5, 2012. Linklater said that, after ten weeks of writing and rehearsing, the film was made in fifteen days for less than $3 million. It premiered at the 2013 Sundance Film Festival in January 2013.

Critical response

Aggregate review scores:

  • Before Sunrise: Rotten Tomatoes: 100% (51 reviews) | Metacritic: 79 (19 reviews)
  • Before Sunset: Rotten Tomatoes: 94% (181 reviews) | Metacritic: 91 (39 reviews)
  • Before Midnight: Rotten Tomatoes: 98% (202 reviews) | Metacritic: 94 (41 reviews)

Before Sunrise

Before Sunrise received high critical praise at the time of its release. Rotten Tomatoes reported that 100% of critics gave the film a positive review based on 51 reviews, with an average rating of 8.4/10. On Metacritic, the film has a weighted average score of 79 out of 100 based on 19 critics, indicating “generally favorable reviews”.

Roger Ebert gave the film three out of four stars. Janet Maslin of The New York Times wrote that it is as uneven as any marathon conversation might be, and Hal Hinson of The Washington Post called it a cut above the banal twentysomething love stories usually seen at the movies.

Before Sunset

Before Sunset received widespread acclaim. On Rotten Tomatoes it holds an approval rating of 94% based on 181 reviews, with an average rating of 8.3/10. On Metacritic, it has a weighted average score of 91 out of 100 based on 39 reviews, indicating “universal acclaim”. The film appeared on 28 critics' top 10 lists of the best films of 2004, and took the 27th spot on Metacritic's list of the best-reviewed movies of the decade (2000-09).

Comparing it to the first film, Ebert wrote that Before Sunset is better, perhaps because the characters are older and wiser and have more to lose or win. Manohla Dargis of the Los Angeles Times called it a deeper, truer work of art than the first.

Before Midnight

Before Midnight also received widespread critical acclaim. Rotten Tomatoes gives it 98% based on 202 critics, with an average rating of 8.7/10, and Metacritic gives it 94 out of 100 based on 41 critics, indicating “universal acclaim”. It was listed as the third-best film of the year after 12 Years a Slave and Gravity, and according to Rotten Tomatoes was the second-best reviewed film of 2013 after Gravity.

Philip Kemp of Total Film compared it to the films of Éric Rohmer, and Perry Seibert of AllMovie praised its long conversations for feeling improvised despite being carefully structured. Eric Kohn of Indiewire added it to his Top 10 Films of 2013. In 2021, The Independent ranked the trilogy third on its list of the 10 greatest movie trilogies of all time.

Accolades

The trilogy received numerous accolades. Linklater won the Silver Bear for Best Director at the 1995 Berlin International Film Festival for Before Sunrise. In 2025, that film was selected by the National Film Preservation Board for preservation in the Library of Congress's National Film Registry as being culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant.

Before Sunset and Before Midnight were both nominated for:

  • the Bodil Award for Best English Language Film
  • the Gotham Independent Film Award for Best Feature
  • the Online Film Critics Society Award for Best Picture
  • the Silver Condor Award for Best Foreign Film

Their screenplays received nominations at the Academy Awards, the Film Independent Spirit Awards, the Online Film Critics Society Awards, and the Writers Guild of America Awards. Delpy was also nominated for the Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture - Musical or Comedy for Before Midnight.
